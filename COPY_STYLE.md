# MotoIndex PH public copy rules

Public copy should sound like a motorcycle reference written by a careful local editor: factual, specific and restrained.

## Default voice
- Lead with the useful fact: price, engine size, seat height, weight, tire size, range or date.
- Prefer numbers and concrete tradeoffs over adjectives.
- Say what is known, what is estimated and what still needs confirmation.
- Use short labels such as `Checked 2026-08-25`, `Estimated` and `Manufacturer-listed` instead of repeatedly announcing that data is “verified.”
- Let the rider decide. Describe differences rather than pretending the site knows the universally correct purchase.

## Avoid public-facing product jargon
Do not expose internal implementation, SEO or data-pipeline language such as:
- canonical page, indexed surface, noindex, source gate, quality gate
- research state, research candidate, source-backed, source-checked
- pipeline, architecture, data layer, compatibility graph, exact edge
- search volume, keyword difficulty, search demand
- launch build, production build, seed record, prototype, version numbers

Those terms may be useful in engineering docs and admin tools, but not in buyer-facing copy.

## Avoid fake precision
- Do not show heuristic recommendation percentages such as `87% match` or `/100` fit scores.
- Do not present a hand-built scoring formula as scientific measurement.
- If a list is ordered, show the factors used and avoid implying that #1 is universally “best.”
- Prefer `Strong on these filters`, `Good on these filters` or a plain list of reasons.

## Avoid generic AI/startup copy
Do not use filler such as:
- “without the guesswork”
- “does the math”
- “one magic number”
- “data leaders”
- “unlock”, “ultimate”, “perfect”, “seamless”, “smart choice”
- “make an informed decision” when the concrete facts can simply be shown

Avoid the repeated pattern `A [adjective] motorcycle that...`. Model summaries should normally start with a useful specification or model status.

## Recommendations and rankings
- Avoid `best` unless the criterion is explicit and measurable.
- Explain the ordering criterion in plain language.
- Do not use search popularity as a buyer-facing recommendation signal.
- Do not turn a spec advantage into a purchase verdict.

Good:
> 157cc automatic scooter weighing 116 kg, with a 778 mm seat and 5.5 L fuel tank.

Bad:
> A light, quick and practical 160-class scooter positioned as a strong value commuter.

## Trust and caveats
Keep caveats where a mistake could matter: finance, tire/accessory fitment, recalls, flooding, fuel-economy estimates and used values. Make them short and specific.

Good:
> Size match only. Confirm load index, speed rating, rim compatibility and clearance.

Avoid repeating generic trust claims. A date and source are stronger than repeatedly saying “verified” or “source-backed.”
